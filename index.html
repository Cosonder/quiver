    Conventions

    In the language of quiver representations: P(i)j has a basis of the paths from i to j (top S(i)), I(i)j has a basis of the paths from j to i (socle S(i)), and S(i) is k at vertex i and 0 elsewhere. dim kQ is the number of all paths, including the trivial paths ei of length 0. R is the ideal generated by the arrows; kQ / R^L sets all paths of length ≥ L to zero. If Q has an oriented cycle, kQ is infinite-dimensional; use truncation instead.

      Help

      Drawing

      • Double-click an empty spot to add a mutable vertex; Shift + double-click adds a frozen vertex (square).
      • Arrow: drag from the source to the target, or click the source and then the target. Repeat to get multiple arrows; an arrow in the opposite direction cancels one (quivers have no 2-cycles).
      • With the Delete tool, clicking an arrow removes one arrow and clicking a vertex removes the vertex.
      • With the Select tool, drag vertices to move them; double-click a vertex to mutate there.
      • The Proof panel in the bottom-right corner explains every step: each edit, each mutation (the three combinatorial steps checked against the matrix formula) and each dimension vector you highlight. The AR quiver window has its own Proof panel showing how the picture is computed.
      • The Path algebra kQ panel on the right gives the dimension of kQ (or of the truncation kQ/RL) and the dimension vectors of the indecomposable projectives P(i), injectives I(i) and simples S(i). Click a row to show the dimension vector on the canvas and the radical / socle layers.
      • AR quiver: for the path algebra kQ of an acyclic quiver, the Auslander–Reiten quiver is computed by knitting. Dynkin types (A/D/E) give all indecomposables; Euclidean types also show the regular components (exceptional and homogeneous tubes); wild types show the first layers of the preprojective and preinjective components. In the window you can also switch to the bounded derived category Db(mod kQ) (ℤQ, with a chosen range of shifts) and the cluster category CQ = Db/τ⁻¹[1] (all objects for Dynkin types, in bijection with cluster variables).

      Mutation μk

      1. For each path i → k → j, add an arrow i → j;
      2. reverse all arrows incident to k;
      3. remove all 2-cycles.

      Matrix form: b′ij = −bij if i = k or j = k, and otherwise b′ij = bij + (|bik|bkj + bik|bkj|)/2.

      Ice quivers

      In an ice quiver some vertices are frozen (squares): you cannot mutate at a frozen vertex, and arrows between frozen vertices are ignored (and removed after mutation). The exchange matrix B̃ keeps only the columns of mutable vertices (its rows are all vertices). “Principal coefficients” adds a frozen vertex i′ and an arrow i → i′ for each mutable vertex i; the columns of the frozen block then give the c-vectors.
